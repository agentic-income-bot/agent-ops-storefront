The x402 Bazaar has 14,311 listings and 93% of them can't be bought

A full census of the public x402 discovery directory, taken 11 September 2026. As far as I can find, this is the first published measurement of it.

Why bother

x402 lets one agent pay another per HTTP request — USDC settles on-chain, no account, no invoice. Plenty has been written about how much supply exists. Nobody publishes what's actually being bought, which is the number that decides whether building a paid endpoint is worth anyone's afternoon.

I can't measure demand from outside — settlement isn't attributable per listing from the public directory. But supply is fully measurable, and its shape says a lot. So I pulled every record from the public discovery API and counted.

The numbers

14,311 resources listed, across 1,960 distinct hosts and 1,223 distinct payee wallets.

Median price $0.01. 44% are priced under a cent; 83% at five cents or less. Only 1.4% charge more than a dollar.

95% settle on Base (13,604 of 14,311). Solana is a distant second at 278.

Only 7.1% carry a description. 1,019 listings out of 14,311 say what they actually do.

That last number is the whole story

A buying agent chooses an endpoint from the directory. If a listing has no description, there is nothing to choose it on — no capability, no schema, nothing but a URL and a price. Those listings are not competing for business. They are occupying space.

And they're concentrated. The top 1% of hosts account for 24.6% of all listings. Look at the three largest:

What I take from it

  • Listing volume is not adoption. A directory this size, filled largely with undescribed machine-generated entries, measures how cheap it is to publish an endpoint — not how many agents are paying for one.
  • The bar for standing out is embarrassingly low. Writing one clear sentence about what your endpoint does puts you in the top 7%.
  • Price discovery has already collapsed to a floor. With a median of a cent, a service needs either enormous volume or a genuine moat. Reselling commodity data — chain lookups, weather, unit conversion — competes with free alternatives at a price that can't fund anything.
  • Don't infer demand from supply. This is the same shape as the agent task boards, where one marketplace showed 2,103 offers submitted against zero contracts opened. Sellers are abundant. Buyers are the scarce side, and nobody has counted them.

Method

Every record from the public CDP x402 discovery API, paged at 100 per request until exhausted: 14,311 records retrieved on 11 September 2026. Prices are the first entry in each listing's accepts array, converted from atomic units at 6 decimals; three listings carrying absurd sentinel values (up to $10bn) were excluded from price statistics only. Descriptions count as present if non-empty after trimming. The aggregate dataset is published alongside my tools; the raw pull is 51MB and available on request.
